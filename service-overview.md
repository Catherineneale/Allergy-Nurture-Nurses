# Allergy Nurture Nurses — Service Overview

Consolidated reference of everything Catherine has shared about the service, for use across future work (website content, outreach materials, documents). Where a source document exists, it's referenced rather than duplicated in full.

## What the service is

Allergy Nurture Nurses (trading name of Allergy Nurture Care Ltd) is a nurse-led, patient-focused advisory service providing advice and support to children and families around Non-IgE food allergy and associated allergic conditions, including eczema, allergic rhinitis and allergy-triggered respiratory asthma.

The service is designed to run alongside NHS services, not replace them, improving accessibility, outcomes and patient choice. It's CQC-registered. Advice is evidence-based, maintained through role-specific training underpinned by research from allergy experts and guidance from NICE and national/global bodies.

The service exists to avoid a postcode lottery: support is available across the UK, including for families whose primary language isn't English. Where NHS services are stretched, the service can reduce pressure on long waiting times for families who can be safely supported in the community, working with primary care to help families access the right service at the right time. Helping families understand their child's nutritional needs (monitoring, education) can also help reduce unnecessary time on restrictive diets or prescription formulas, reducing financial burden on GPs.

## Service model

National remote/virtual nurse-led allergy and associated allergic conditions advisory service, with occasional Essex-based in-person consultations if required, conducted in a secure, appropriately risk-assessed space or educational setting.

## Positioning decisions (confirmed by Catherine)

- **Primarily a children's service.** Not routinely advertising adult support separately. Wider family support may be offered where appropriate, in the context of supporting the family as a whole (which may include general advice for adults within the family) — but this stays contextual, not a headline offering.
- **Not over-promising on training.** As of September 2026, no formal allergy/asthma awareness training package for nurseries/schools is set up yet, even though it's referenced as a future service area. Outreach materials should lead with family support, not training, until the package exists.

## Scope of practice, inclusion and exclusion criteria

Full detail lives in `policies/Allergy_Nurture_Care_Ltd_Inclusion_and_Exclusion_Criteria.docx` (source document, provided by Catherine — use this as the authoritative wording, not paraphrase). Key points:

- **Included:** children, young people (0–17) and families (adults where appropriate — see positioning note above) with mild-to-moderate suspected/diagnosed allergic conditions: Non-IgE mediated food allergy, suspected food-related symptoms, eczema with possible allergic triggers, infant feeding concerns (reflux, colic, constipation where allergy suspected), allergic rhinitis.
- **Excluded:** known/suspected IgE-mediated food allergy requiring specialist medical management, history of anaphylaxis, acute allergic reactions, severe/uncontrolled asthma, significant respiratory compromise, anything requiring diagnostic testing, prescribing, or urgent/emergency care.
- **Escalation:** 999, NHS 111, or the patient's GP as appropriate.
- **Does not provide:** diagnostic testing, prescribing/administration of medicines, emergency care. Offers advisory prescription pathways where appropriate (with consent), liaising with the child's GP on recommended prescriptions where clinically necessary. Does not formally diagnose, but supports understanding; liaises with the GP where formal diagnosis or specialist referral is needed.
- **Multidisciplinary sharing (with consent):** GP, health visitor, school nursing team, dietitian, other relevant professionals.

## Accessibility

Full detail lives in `policies/Accessible_Information_Standard_Statement.docx` (source document). Key points: compliant with the NHS Accessible Information Standard (DCB1605) and Equality Act 2010 reasonable adjustments duty, underpinned by the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 and CQC Fundamental Standards. Five-step AIS process: Identify, Record, Flag, Share, Meet. Reasonable adjustments may include flexible appointment length, alternative communication methods, interpreter services, written summaries, involving carers/advocates. Consent and capacity assessed via the Mental Capacity Act 2005 and Gillick competency principles.

## Regulatory and professional framework

- CQC-registered; assessed against safe, effective, caring, responsive, well-led.
- NMC Code (2018): prioritise people, practise effectively, preserve safety, promote professionalism and trust.
- Guiding framework: care, compassion, competence, communication, courage, commitment.
- Evidence base drawn from NICE, BSACI, Allergy UK, National Eczema Society, Asthma + Lung UK.

## Verified statistics (with sources, checked September 2026)

- **40%** of UK children have a diagnosed allergy — Allergy UK.
- **10–15%** of UK children have allergic rhinitis — BSACI.
- **348%** rise in child (5–14) asthma hospital admissions every September vs. August — Asthma + Lung UK (a 2024 update put this as high as 408%; 348% is the more commonly cited figure).
- **54** child deaths from asthma and **19** from anaphylaxis in England, April 2019–March 2023 — National Child Mortality Database (NCMD).
