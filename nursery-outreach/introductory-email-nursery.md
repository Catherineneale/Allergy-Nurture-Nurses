I am writing to introduce Allergy Nurture Nurses, a CQC-registered, nurse-led remote allergy and asthma advisory service supporting children, young people and families across the UK.

The service has been established to provide timely, accessible advice and education for families navigating Non-IgE food allergy and associated allergic conditions, including eczema, allergic rhinitis and allergy-related respiratory/asthma concerns.

I am reaching out to nurseries and early years settings to introduce Allergy Nurture Nurses and explore whether the service could support your team and the families in your care. My aim is to understand how nurse-led allergy and asthma education could help build staff confidence, and how individual families with allergy-related concerns might be supported alongside your existing policies and procedures.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss:

how nurse-led allergy and asthma awareness training could build your team's confidence, understanding and day-to-day safety when caring for children with allergic conditions;
whether families in your setting who are experiencing uncertainty about a child's allergy-related symptoms could benefit from early education, signposting and non-urgent advisory support;
how Allergy Nurture Nurses could work alongside your existing individual healthcare plans and safeguarding procedures, without replacing them;
opportunities for ongoing collaboration as new children with allergy-related needs join your setting.

Allergy Nurture Nurses is led by Catherine Neale, Registered Children's Nurse and Clinical Nurse Specialist, with over 25 years' nursing experience, including the last 7 years working in specialist paediatric allergy. The service is underpinned by ongoing role-specific training, continuing professional development and evidence-informed practice, drawing on current guidance and standards from organisations including NICE, BSACI, Allergy UK, National Eczema Society, Asthma + Lung UK, CQC and the NMC.

The purpose of Allergy Nurture Nurses is not to replace your setting's own medical or safeguarding procedures, but to work alongside them — helping families access clear information and practical support, so that the information reaching your team about a child's needs is more consistent and better understood.

The service is particularly focused on families who may be experiencing uncertainty, confusion or delay in understanding their child's symptoms. Unclear or inconsistent information at home can make it harder for your team to manage a child's needs confidently and safely — through a focused allergy history, education and practical support, Allergy Nurture Nurses can help families understand what information matters and how to navigate their child's allergy journey with more confidence.

The service operates within a clear scope of practice. Allergy Nurture Nurses provides advisory and educational support only. We offer advisory prescription pathways where appropriate and with patient and/or parent consent, liaising with the child's GP around recommended prescriptions where clinically necessary. Allergy Nurture Nurses does not formally diagnose, but offers evidence-based advice and support to give families a clearer understanding of their child's health conditions. Where a formal diagnosis is required, we liaise with the child's GP to support a multidisciplinary approach to care. We do not provide allergy testing, but where clinically relevant we will liaise with the child's GP for referral to secondary care specialist teams. We do not replace GP/NHS/specialist care, and we do not provide urgent or emergency treatment — children remain under their GP, NHS team or relevant healthcare professionals for diagnosis, prescribing, treatment and urgent care, and your setting's own emergency procedures remain unchanged.

Our inclusion criteria include children and families seeking non-urgent support with suspected or known Non-IgE food allergy, eczema where allergy may be part of the wider picture, allergic rhinitis, allergy-related respiratory/asthma awareness, feeding concerns, food introduction and practical symptom education.

Our exclusion criteria include any child who is acutely unwell, experiencing breathing difficulty, symptoms of anaphylaxis, acute asthma symptoms, collapse, severe illness or rapid deterioration. In these circumstances, families and settings are advised to seek urgent medical help via 999 or emergency care, in line with your existing emergency procedures.

Allergy Nurture Nurses has a clear accessibility statement and policy available on request and located on the website, reflecting our commitment to supporting families whose primary language is not English or who have additional communication needs.
