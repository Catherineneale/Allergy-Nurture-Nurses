Baby Weaning and Food Allergy: Introducing Allergenic Foods with Confidence

Starting weaning is an exciting milestone, but for many families it also brings a wave of questions and worry, especially around allergenic foods.

Parents are often told that early introduction of allergenic foods matters, but are left unsure how, when or in what order to do this safely. If there is a family history of allergy, eczema, or a sibling with a known food allergy, that worry can feel even bigger.

At Allergy Nurture Nurses, we understand that weaning should feel like a positive, confident experience for families, not an anxious one. We support parents to introduce allergenic foods in a safe, informed and manageable way.

What do we mean by allergenic foods?
Allergenic foods are foods that are more commonly associated with allergic reactions in children. These typically include cow's milk, egg, peanut, tree nuts, wheat, soya, sesame, fish and shellfish.

Current guidance supports introducing allergenic foods from around six months of age, alongside breast milk or formula, and continuing to offer them regularly as part of a varied diet. Delaying introduction is not generally recommended and is not known to reduce allergy risk.

Weaning babies with eczema or a family history of allergy
Babies with moderate to severe eczema, or those with a parent or sibling with a food allergy, may be at higher risk of developing a food allergy. This does not mean allergenic foods should be avoided. In fact, introducing them can be part of supporting a baby's long-term health.

Allergy Nurture Nurses can help families understand their baby's individual risk, talk through a practical introduction plan and feel more confident about what to expect.

Introducing allergenic foods one at a time
Many parents ask whether allergenic foods need to be introduced one at a time, and how long to wait between new foods. Understanding what a reaction might look like, and what is simply normal newborn or infant behaviour, can make this process feel far less daunting.

We help families build a clear, step-by-step approach that fits around their baby's feeding, sleep and daily routine, rather than a rigid or overwhelming checklist.

Recognising a possible reaction during weaning
Reactions to food can vary. Some appear quickly, such as hives, swelling or vomiting, while others, particularly with Non-IgE food allergy, can be delayed and easily missed.

Knowing what to look out for, and what to record, can help families feel more prepared and less anxious as they introduce new foods.

If your baby has swelling of the lips, face or throat, difficulty breathing, persistent vomiting, becomes floppy or pale, or you suspect anaphylaxis, seek urgent medical help immediately by calling 999 or attending emergency care.

What Allergy Nurture Nurses does and does not provide
Allergy Nurture Nurses is a CQC-registered, nurse-led allergy and asthma advisory service for children, young people and families.

We provide education, support, practical guidance and signposting. We do not diagnose, prescribe, carry out allergy testing or provide emergency care.

Your baby or child should remain under their GP, NHS team or relevant healthcare professionals for diagnosis, medical treatment, prescribing and urgent care.
